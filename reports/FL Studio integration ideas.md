# Ex-Machina already outclasses the official route; FLkey sets the bar

Native Instruments and Image-Line never made an FL Studio integration for the Mikro MK3. The only official recipe gives you transport over Mackie Control and loses the pads. That means the integrations to measure Ex-Machina against are Novation's FLkey 2, Image-Line's Akai Fire script and the DrivenByMoss Bitwig extension, not NI. Measured against those three, Ex-Machina already covers the core most users ask for: transport, undo, channel select/mute/solo, a 16-step sequencer, pattern jumping, mixer volume/pan, plug-in parameters, presets and the browser. It also has hardware access no Mikro script has had before: Shift, the arrows, screen text and four-level LEDs. The biggest gaps are these. First, per-step editing: holding a step and turning a control to set its velocity, pitch or pan is the feature reviewers praise most on the Fire and FLkey. Second, the one-press FL actions FLkey users get: Score Log dump, quick quantize, loop record and count-in. Third, the 25 strip LEDs are unused as a value display. Fourth, sliced-instrument (Slicex) layouts and Channel Rack group awareness. Fifth, a colour pipeline that is guessed rather than measured. The colours look "off" because the script matches FL colours to the device's 16 hues by **estimated hue angles alone**. It ignores saturation and value, and the RGB byte order has never been checked. Selected items are shown as "brighter same hue", but the firmware renders only two visible brightness steps. The Fire's HSV pipeline and FLkey's "selected = white" rule point to a measured palette table as the fix. The prioritised list at the end puts that fix first, followed by step editing and a handful of cheap one-press actions.

## The official route stops at five transport buttons

There is no vendor-made FL Studio integration for the Mikro MK3, the Maschine MK3 or the Maschine+. Image-Line's controller page lists "Maschine Micro MK3" only as a link to a community forum thread, and it notes that user scripts are untested by Image-Line ([Image-Line: MIDI Controllers](https://www.image-line.com/fl-studio/midi-controllers)). That thread holds a Controller Editor template, not a script, and its author was still hoping for official support ([Image-Line forum t=213312](https://forum.image-line.com/viewtopic.php?p=1438864)). NI's own article tells users to load the generic **Mackie Control** template and pick FL's Mackie Control Universal type ([NI support 69000879800](https://support.native-instruments.com/support/solutions/articles/69000879800-setting-up-your-maschine-controller-for-host-transport-control-in-fl-studio)). Inside that template, read from the Controller Editor 2.8.2 factory content, **only Play, Stop, Restart, Erase and Tap** send MCU messages. Rec is disabled, and pad page A sends no notes at all ([NI drivers page](https://support.native-instruments.com/support/solutions/articles/69000882238-native-instruments-drivers-other-files)). NI made FL templates for the MK1, the Mikro MK1 and the Jam, but nothing for the MK3 generation.

The community has filled the gap thinly. One user ("aram") wrote a transport script that still needs a Controller Editor profile. He found that "Shift button and the arrow < > buttons are not sending a midi message", could not get the transport LEDs to light, and summed up the vendors' answer as "Just use mackie" ([Image-Line forum p=1755264](https://forum.image-line.com/viewtopic.php?p=1755264)). The banks in the template thread show what users reach for first: FPC, FPC at velocity 100-127, Omni channel-rack triggering, and undo/redo on the encoder ([Image-Line forum t=213312](https://forum.image-line.com/viewtopic.php?p=1438864)). Ex-Machina covers all of that without NI software. It also fixes the three things stock MIDI mode cannot do: Shift and the arrows send MIDI, the script writes the screen, and LEDs take host colours. Even DrivenByMoss, the most complete Mikro integration on any DAW, has to use Stop as a fake Shift. It never writes the screen, and it treats button LEDs as on/off ([DrivenByMoss Mikro Mk3 doc](https://github.com/git-moss/DrivenByMoss-Documentation/blob/master/Native-Instruments/Native-Instruments-Maschine-Mikro-Mk3.md); [Maschine.java](https://github.com/git-moss/DrivenByMoss/blob/master/src/main/java/de/mossgrabers/controller/ni/maschine/Maschine.java)).

Sources for the table: NI's template contents ([NI drivers page](https://support.native-instruments.com/support/solutions/articles/69000882238-native-instruments-drivers-other-files)), the DrivenByMoss documentation and source ([Mikro Mk3 doc](https://github.com/git-moss/DrivenByMoss-Documentation/blob/master/Native-Instruments/Native-Instruments-Maschine-Mikro-Mk3.md); [ShiftView.java](https://github.com/git-moss/DrivenByMoss/blob/master/src/main/java/de/mossgrabers/controller/ni/maschine/mk3/view/ShiftView.java); [TouchstripCommand.java](https://github.com/git-moss/DrivenByMoss/blob/master/src/main/java/de/mossgrabers/controller/ni/maschine/mk3/command/continuous/TouchstripCommand.java)), and Novation's FLkey 2 guides ([FLkey 2 37 guide](https://fael-downloads-prod.focusrite.com/customer/prod/downloads/flkey_2_37_user_guide_v5_en.pdf); [FLkey 2 Mini guide](https://fael-downloads-prod.focusrite.com/customer/prod/downloads/flkey_2_mini_25_user_guide_v9_en.pdf)). The Fire's transport flags, step paging and value bars come from the script installed on this Mac (`device_Fire.py`, version 2026.4), whose header points to the [Image-Line forum](https://forum.image-line.com/viewtopic.php?p=1496543#p1496543).

Two FLkey details translate directly to a 16-pad device. The first is **hold a step, turn an encoder**. In Sequencer mode, holding steps turns encoders 1-8 into Note Pitch, Velocity, Fine Pitch, Panning, Mod X, Mod Y, Shift and Repeat. FL opens the Graph Editor while the step is held. Holding for more than a second latches a multi-step selection ([FLkey 2 37 guide p.27-28](https://fael-downloads-prod.focusrite.com/customer/prod/downloads/flkey_2_37_user_guide_v5_en.pdf)). The Mikro has one encoder, so it needs a parameter selector. Its **Events** button is free in the FL Studio mapping, and in Maschine it means exactly this: editing note events. DrivenByMoss already uses Events for note-parameter editing on the same hardware ([Mikro Mk3 doc](https://github.com/git-moss/DrivenByMoss-Documentation/blob/master/Native-Instruments/Native-Instruments-Maschine-Mikro-Mk3.md)). The second detail is **one-press FL actions**. Score Log dumps the last five minutes of played notes into the selected pattern. Shift + Score Log quick-quantizes start times. The script turns Loop Record on at the first connection, so that recording loops the pattern instead of extending it ([FLkey 2 37 guide p.33-35](https://fael-downloads-prod.focusrite.com/customer/prod/downloads/flkey_2_37_user_guide_v5_en.pdf)). Each of these is a single FL API call. The Shift layer of the FL Studio mapping still has room for them: Shift with Rec, Stop, Restart, Follow, Duplicate, Select, Solo, Group, Scene, Events or Variation has no shifted action in `mmk3_config._fl_studio()` (action 6, "keeps its normal action"). With Shift held, those buttons send their normal CC, so the script cannot tell Shift + Rec from Rec. Giving one a Shift function means adding a CC to `FL_SHIFT_CC`. The FL Studio mapping is built into the firmware at `CFG_FL_ADDR`, so that change needs a firmware build and a flash, not just a script update.

## Users praise plug-and-play and step entry, and punish fragility

Across reviews and forums, praise lands on the same few things. FLkey reviews call the integration "superb" out of the box. They praise the Chord / Scale / Arp modes, step sequencing straight into the Channel Rack, and picking the instrument to play without the mouse ([MusicTech FLkey 2 review](https://musictech.com/reviews/controllers/novation-flkey-2-review/); [Thomann FLkey Mini reviews](https://www.thomann.de/lu/novation_flkey_mini_reviews.htm)). The Fire is praised for step sequencing "much faster than clicking steps with a mouse". Reviewers also like pad brightness that shows velocity, and holding a pad while turning an encoder to edit that step ([Digital DJ Tips Fire review](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/reviews/akai-pro-fire/)). Ex-Machina already has the scale, chord and arp help on the device and a silent, mouse-free channel select. Hold-a-step editing is the praised feature it does not have.

The complaints are just as consistent, and several point at choices Ex-Machina must not repeat. On every FLkey, **selecting a channel plays a note**, which pushes users back to the mouse ([Image-Line forum p=2060128](https://forum.image-line.com/viewtopic.php?p=2060128)). Ex-Machina's Select grid swallows the pad note, so this complaint is already avoided. Keep it that way, and put any preview behind a modifier. Mixing one channel at a time on encoders is "slower than the mouse" ([Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/reviews/akai-pro-fire/)). That hits a single-encoder device hardest, so a value display and automatic focus matter more here than on the FLkey. Fire users report that the Fire has only **two velocity levels** and a weak drum mode ([MusicRadar Fire review](https://musicradar.com/reviews/akai-professional-fire)). The Mikro's velocity pads are an edge that the step sequencer does not use yet, because a pad hit only toggles a step. Community scripts that **break after FL or Windows updates** recur. NFX scripts fail to initialise on Windows 11 24H2 ([Image-Line forum p=1993711](https://forum.image-line.com/viewtopic.php?p=1993711)), and even Image-Line's built-in Fire script glitched in FL 26.1.2 ([Image-Line forum p=2068160](https://forum.image-line.com/viewtopic.php?p=2068160)). Ex-Machina's `call()` wrapper already turns missing functions into defaults. But an exception in the script's own arithmetic or drawing would still stop the script with the device in host mode, showing a frozen screen. Channel Rack groups are a known trap too: Fire users found that source switching broke with groups and that Omni mode wrapped at channel 17 ([Image-Line forum p=1500952](https://forum.image-line.com/viewtopic.php?p=1500952); [p=1327799](https://forum.image-line.com/viewtopic.php?p=1327799)). FLkey avoids this by **following FL's group drop-down** ([FLkey 2 37 guide p.22-24](https://fael-downloads-prod.focusrite.com/customer/prod/downloads/flkey_2_37_user_guide_v5_en.pdf)).

On requests, users ask for clip launching: an FLkey Mini owner could not trigger Performance-mode clips ([Image-Line forum p=1840540](https://forum.image-line.com/viewtopic.php?p=1840540)). The Fire has a Perform mode, and Image-Line's original Mikro MK1 mapping was built around clip launching with SCENE ([Image-Line forum p=932083](https://forum.image-line.com/viewtopic.php?p=932083)). Image-Line's newest controller, FLpad, codifies the target feature list. It step-sequences up to four instruments, has FPC / Slicex / Fruity Slicer layouts, scale-locked notes, a Channel Rack mixer with pads as faders, pattern create/clear/duplicate, and Graph Editor note editing ([Image-Line news: FLpad](https://www.image-line.com/news/out-now-flpad-the-grid-controller-built-for-fl-studio)). No source mentions anyone using the Mikro's screen with FL, because stock MIDI mode never allowed it. That leaves Ex-Machina's screen as a differentiator with no competitor to copy. FLkey's pattern of a resting status screen plus short name-and-value overlays with a user-set timeout is the closest model ([FLkey 2 Mini guide p.64](https://fael-downloads-prod.focusrite.com/customer/prod/downloads/flkey_2_mini_25_user_guide_v9_en.pdf)).

## The pad colours are off because hue alone is guessed

The current pipeline (`exm_out.nearest`) splits FL's integer as `0xRRGGBB` and computes the hue. It returns white when the colour is very dark or its chroma is under 25 % of its maximum. Otherwise it picks the nearest of sixteen hue angles: `0, 20, 30, 42, 55, 80, 120, 150, 180, 200, 240, 258, 272, 288, 305, 330`. The code calls them "the hue (degrees) of each of the device's colours", but nobody measured them; they were estimated from the colour names. Four problems stack up.

First, **the angles are unmeasured, and an independent mapping disagrees with them**. The real unit confirmed only the palette's order (`docs/midi-patch.md`). DrivenByMoss sends the same index space through NI's MIDI mode, with hand-made names for each block. It calls the block we call "turquoise, 200°" its "blue" and sends Bitwig's blue there. It calls our "cyan" block "sky". Bitwig's purple goes to our "plum" block, and Bitwig's pink to our "magenta" ([MaschineColorManager.java](https://github.com/git-moss/DrivenByMoss/blob/master/src/main/java/de/mossgrabers/controller/ni/maschine/core/MaschineColorManager.java)). Its names for the green-side blocks ("spring", "green", "lime") are not even in hue order, so it is not an authority either. The disagreement shows that the cool half of the palette, where FL's default blues and purples live, is where a guessed table goes wrong. LED palettes are rarely spaced evenly in hue: this one spends five entries between red and yellow.

Second, **hue-only matching throws away saturation and value**. FL's channel and pattern colours are often muted. A desaturated brown and a vivid orange land on the same full-saturation LED, and a dark navy lights as bright blue. The Fire script handles this explicitly. It starts from `channels.getChannelColor`, converts to HSV, scales by per-user brightness and saturation, keeps a minimum value of 0.1 for lit pads, and replaces FL's default sampler grey with near-white `0xC8C8C8` (`device_Fire.py`, [Image-Line forum](https://forum.image-line.com/viewtopic.php?p=1496543#p1496543)). The Fire has true RGB pads, so it never has to quantise. Ex-Machina does, so it should map saturation to "white or hue" and value to brightness level, rather than discard both.

Third, **brightness is effectively two steps**. The firmware documentation says levels 0/1 are dim and 2/3 are bright (`docs/midi-patch.md`). The script draws unselected items at level 1 and selected at 3, a dim-versus-bright pair of the same hue. That reads as "everything looks dark and slightly wrong", because dim LEDs also shift in perceived hue. FLkey takes a different approach: base colours come from FL objects, and **the selected or playing item is white** ([FLkey 2 37 guide p.21-24](https://fael-downloads-prod.focusrite.com/customer/prod/downloads/flkey_2_37_user_guide_v5_en.pdf)). DrivenByMoss uses the dim step only for "queued" or "continuation" states ([ClipView.java](https://github.com/git-moss/DrivenByMoss/blob/master/src/main/java/de/mossgrabers/controller/ni/maschine/mk3/view/ClipView.java)).

Fourth, **the byte order is unverified**. `docs/fl-studio.md` lists the colour order of `getChannelColor` as an open question. If FL hands the script `0xBBGGRR` for some objects, red and blue swap, and every hue lands far from where it should.

The fix follows from these four points. Measure the palette once: light each of the 16 colours at levels 1 and 3 (the firmware's `mmk3_host.py colors` demo already does this). Record an approximate RGB per entry by eye against a reference, or from a phone photo. Store it as a table in `exm_tables.py` and match in a perceptual space such as CIELAB or OKLab instead of by hue angle. Then apply the Fire's rules: low saturation goes to white, low value goes to dim, FL's default grey becomes white, and user-adjustable brightness and saturation offsets live in a settings block. Adopt FLkey's state rules: unselected items at the bright level in their own hue, selected and playhead in white, step-sequencer inactive steps dim in the channel colour rather than off. Add a one-shot **colour test**, for example a Shift combination on Variation. It would set the selected channel's colour to pure red, then green, then blue, and show the measured RGB and chosen index on the screen. That settles the byte-order question in one session on the real device.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "what is missing compared with NI" to "what makes FL-dedicated controllers feel native". The official answer for the MK3 generation is close to nothing, so Ex-Machina competes with FLkey 2 and the Fire. Their reputations rest less on breadth than on tight feedback loops: the selected item turns white, navigation lights only where it can go, and holding a step turns the controls into that step's editor. Ex-Machina has more feedback hardware than either rival in one respect, with a writable text screen, 25 strip LEDs and velocity pads. What it lacks is the feedback discipline that makes that hardware legible. That is why the colour work and step editing lead the list, ahead of new modes such as clip launching.

The colour problem is a measurement problem, not a design flaw, and it is the cheapest high-impact fix. It also has a side benefit: a measured palette table is reusable by any future DAW script, including a Bitwig port built on DrivenByMoss's mode design, whose LGPL licence allows reuse with conditions. Hardening the callbacks and keeping channel select silent cost almost nothing. Those are the points where community scripts have most often failed their users.
